# P11 Experiment Report: Targeted OCR Escalation Evaluation (T42)

## Executive Summary
- **Experiment ID**: P11
- **Task ID**: T42 (pdf-t42)
- **Status**: `completed`
- **Disposition**: `rejected_experiment`
- **Recommendation**: Reject padded selected-region retry escalation as an automated OCR recovery strategy. While padding can unclip partially cropped glyphs in isolation, on tightly spaced structured forms it captures adjacent neighbor fields into the target recognition box, causing fatal field corruption and unacceptable boundary precision loss.

## Evaluated Mechanisms
1. **Baseline**: Single selected-page/region OCR without retry.
   - Evaluated on F14 (`native-unicode`), F15 (`ocr-material`), and F16 (`adjacent-crop`).
   - Cleanly respects user-selected region boundaries without corrupting neighboring field contents.
   - On partially clipped selections (`adjacent-crop-clipped.pdf`), fails to recover characters cut off by the user's boundary (e.g. `$100` recognized as `100` missing the leading currency symbol).
2. **Selected Candidate**: Padded selected/mismatched-region retry with inverse transform.
   - Applies context padding (8 raster pixels or 10% of region height) and retries recognition.
   - On `adjacent-crop-clipped.pdf`, unclips the leading `$`, successfully recovering the full target amount `$100`.
   - **Fatal Neighbor Corruption (F16)**: On `adjacent-crop-adjacent.pdf`, the neighboring field `$200` begins only 8 points past the target boundary (x=135 vs x=150). The padded retry captures the neighbor into the target crop, recognizing `$100 $200` instead of isolating the target field.

## Architectural Conclusion
The experiment criteria explicitly require:
1. `clean neighbor fields not corrupted`
2. `reject if gains require clipping neighbors or more false alerts`
3. `>=20% useful target gains at <=1pp precision loss or reject`

Because the padded retry mechanism captures neighboring fields on tightly packed financial and tabular layouts, it fails the mandatory negative controls. The candidate is formally **rejected**. Targeted region extraction must preserve strict user selection boundaries without automated spatial expansion.
